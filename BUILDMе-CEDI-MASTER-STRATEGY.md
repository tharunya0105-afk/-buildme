# PART 1 — RUTHLESS HONEST ASSESSMENT

## Can BuildMe survive a CEDI evaluation?

**Honest answer: Yes — but only if you reposition and focus ruthlessly.**

BuildMe's current prototype has genuine technical depth (30+ database models, explainable risk engine, evidence-linked timeline, GPS geofencing, quotation analysis). But it suffers from **feature sprawl** — 78 pages across too many concepts (EIR Dashboard, Commercialization Command Center, Validation page, Spatial Analytics, Property Intelligence, AI Intelligence Center, etc.). A CEDI evaluator will see a student who built many things rather than a founder who solved one problem deeply.

The saving grace: **the core insight — connecting PLAN → EVIDENCE → CHANGE → MONEY — is genuinely differentiated and not served by existing Indian construction software.**

The strategic task is not to add more. It is to **strip away everything that doesn't strengthen that core loop and make it undeniable in a 3-minute demo.**

# PART 3 — THE 17 CEDI QUESTIONS

## Q1: What exactly is the problem?

**20-second answer:**
"Homeowners pay construction costs month by month without understanding what changed, why it changed, or whether the money they're paying matches what was actually built."

**60-second answer:**
"Indian residential construction typically costs ₹20–80 lakh. Homeowners receive a quotation, make payments as requested, and trust the engineer's explanation. When the budget increases — which research shows happens in most projects — homeowners often discover it only after money is nearly exhausted. BuildMe connects every important construction event — quotation, change, evidence, payment, progress — into one transparent timeline that answers: 'What happened to my house and my money?'"

**Evidence:** 15 survey responses confirmed budget overruns, difficulty tracking payments, confusion about what photos mean, and inability to understand budget changes. 5 real quotations showed that scope differences make simple price comparison misleading.

**What not to say:** "Construction is expensive" or "there are no good construction apps."

**Likely follow-up:** "Is this really a problem, or just inconvenience?"
**Defense:** "When a homeowner discovers a ₹3 lakh budget increase after it has already been spent, that is not inconvenience — it is a financial trust breakdown. Our survey showed this is a recurring, financially material problem."

## Q2: Who is your first paying customer?

**20-second answer:**
"The civil engineer managing 3–10 residential projects, who is tired of answering the same progress and budget questions repeatedly."

**60-second answer:**
"The civil engineer is the first payer because they (1) manage the project, (2) handle the money, (3) need to communicate with homeowners, (4) already pay for tools (WhatsApp, notebooks, Excel), and (5) directly benefit from reduced repetitive communication. BuildMe replaces the engineer's need to repeatedly explain 'what happened, why it cost more, and what was built' — with a single transparent dashboard."

**Evidence:** The civil engineer interview confirmed: he wants to show daily photos, worker counts, labour costs, quotation amount, amount collected, and progress — directly through an app. He repeatedly explained that clear change communication reduces homeowner tension.

**What not to say:** "Homeowners" (they don't manage the project) or "everyone" (no focus).

**Likely follow-up:** "Why would an engineer pay for something that creates more work?"
**Defense:** "BuildMe doesn't create more work — it converts existing verbal/WhatsApp communication into a structured, reusable timeline. Every update the engineer already sends becomes a permanent evidence record. The engineer saves time explaining the same things repeatedly."
