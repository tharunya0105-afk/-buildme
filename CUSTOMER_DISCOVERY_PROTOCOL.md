# BuildMe — Customer Discovery Protocol

**Objective**: Understand how civil engineers currently handle construction estimation and budget tracking.

**NOT to sell BuildMe.** The goal is learning.

---

## Interview Setup

### Duration
15–20 minutes maximum.

### Participant
Civil engineer / construction professional with residential project experience.

### Setting
In-person preferred. Phone/video acceptable.

### Recording
Written notes only (with permission). No audio recording unless explicitly consented.

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

Do NOT immediately pitch BuildMe.

> "Hi, I'm [name]. I'm researching how construction cost estimation and budget tracking are currently handled by engineers. I want to understand your actual workflow before showing you anything. This will take about 15 minutes. Is now a good time?"

If they agree, proceed.

---

## Section A: Current Workflow (5 minutes)

### A1. Estimation Process
> "How do you currently prepare a construction cost estimate for a residential project?"

**Probe**: What information do you start with? What tools do you use?

### A2. Rate Sources
> "Where do your material and labour rates come from?"

**Probe**: Do you use government schedules? Market rates? Contractor quotes? How often do these rates change?

### A3. Location Factor
> "How do you account for location differences? For example, does a project in Coimbatore cost differently than one in Chennai?"

**Probe**: What specific factors change? Material availability? Labour rates? Transportation?

### A4. Time Factor
> "How do you handle price changes over time? For example, if TMT bar prices increase 10% between your estimate and the actual purchase?"

**Probe**: Do you include escalation clauses? Contingency? How much?

### A5. Estimate vs Quotation
> "When you receive a contractor quotation, how do you compare it with your own estimate?"

**Probe**: What do you look for first? What makes you suspicious of a quote?

---

## Section B: Problems (5 minutes)

### B1. Time Consumption
> "What part of the estimation process takes the most time?"

**Probe**: Is it gathering rates? Doing calculations? Comparing options? Something else?

### B2. Common Errors
> "Where do construction estimates most commonly go wrong?"

**Probe**: Can you give me a recent example? What was the impact?

### B3. Budget Overruns
> "What typically causes a project to exceed its original estimate?"

**Probe**: Is it material price changes? Scope changes? Poor initial estimation? Site conditions?

### B4. Quotation Comparison
> "When two contractor quotations differ significantly for the same project, how do you determine which is more realistic?"

**Probe**: Do you have a systematic method? Or is it experience-based?

### B5. Information Gaps
> "What information do you wish you had when preparing an estimate that you currently don't have access to?"

---

## Section C: Existing Tools (3 minutes)

### C1. Current Software
> "What software or tools do you currently use for estimation or project tracking?"

**Probe**: Excel? Specific software? Calculator? Paper?

### C2. Pain Points
> "What do you dislike about your current tools?"

**Probe**: What would you change if you could?

### C3. Manual Work
> "What parts of your workflow are still manual that you wish were automated?"

---

## Section D: Buying Behavior (3 minutes)

### D1. Decision Maker
> "When a new tool is introduced, who decides whether to adopt it? Is it you, your firm, or your clients?"

### D2. Willingness to Pay
> "If a tool saved you [X hours per estimate / improved accuracy / reduced overruns], would you pay for it? What would be a reasonable price?"

**Probe**: Monthly subscription? Per-project? One-time?

### D3. Trust Factors
> "What would make you trust a cost estimate from a software tool? What would make you distrust it?"

---

## Section E: Validation (2 minutes)

### E1. Pilot Interest
> "I'm building a tool that tracks estimate → quotation → actual project cost. Would you be willing to test it on one project at no cost?"

**If yes**: "What would be a good project to start with?"

**If no**: "What would need to change for you to be interested?"

### E2. Referral
> "Do you know other engineers who face similar estimation challenges? Would you be willing to introduce me?"

---

## Closing (1 minute)

> "Thank you for your time. This has been very helpful. Is it okay if I follow up with you in a few weeks to share what I've learned?"

**Do NOT show BuildMe during the discovery interview.** Save the demonstration for a follow-up.
